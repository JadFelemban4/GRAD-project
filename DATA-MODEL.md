# DATA-MODEL.md — what is in the data, and where each number came from

Three generated files, one raw source. **Never edit `data/` or `logs/raw/` by
hand.** `data/` is regenerated by `build_dataset.py`; `logs/raw/` is measurement.

---

## The pipeline

```
logs/raw/*.csv          BimmerLink exports. Measurement. NEVER EDIT.
        |
        |  build_dataset.py "logs/raw/*.csv"
        v
data/manifest.csv        8 rows    one row per drive
data/master_points.csv  22 rows    steady operating points, pooled, de-duplicated
data/master_samples.csv 46707 rows per-sample rows with derived physics
        |
        |  compare_log.py data/master_points.csv
        v
1.4 % load residual, PASS  (k derived; 2.8 % with k fitted)
```

Regenerating is always safe and always the right move. The outputs are the union
of whatever you pass in, rebuilt from scratch.

## `data/manifest.csv` — one row per drive

13 columns. What Chapter 3 cites when it says how much data exists.

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `file` | source CSV name |
| `samples` | rows in the raw export |
| `duration_s`, `duration_min` | wall-clock span |
| `rate_hz` | **average** sample rate — see the warning below |
| `channels_present` | how many of the expected channels this drive carries |
| `steady_windows` | operating points this drive contributed (before pooling) |
| `max_rpm`, `max_speed_kmh`, `max_load_pct`, `max_map_kpa` | envelope reached |
| `min_lambda` | richest point |
| `warm_samples` | samples above the warm-running threshold |

> **`rate_hz` is an average and can lie.** A drive that dropped a quarter of its
> samples reports a plausible-looking low rate. Check `steady_windows` and the
> rejection counts, not the rate.

## `data/master_points.csv` — 22 steady operating points

34 columns. **This is the input to `compare_log.py`** and the evidence behind the
1.4 % residual. What that residual does and does not measure: mistake 12.

### Measured, straight off the car

`rpm` · `v_kmh` · `air_kgh` · `air2_kgh` · `air_comb` · `load_pct` · `map_raw` ·
`boost` · `p_amb` · `t_amb` · `iat_pre` · `iat_amb` · `ect` · `rad_out` · `oil` ·
`tps` · `gear` · `lam` · `spark` · `wg` · `brake` · `spk_tgt` · `oil_filt` ·
`bst_tgt` · `trq_whl`

> **`map_raw` is the pre-throttle sensor and must not be used as a load input.**
> It is carried so the thesis can reproduce the failure. See `Context.md`.

### Derived

| column | how |
|---|---|
| `air_gps` | `air_kgh` converted, kg/h → g/s |
| `map_kpa` | **`plant.map_from_airflow(air_gps, rpm, iat_k)`** — inverted from air mass, *not* read from the pressure channel |
| `corr_flow` | `plant.corrected_flow(...)`, compressor-map normalisation |
| `press_ratio` | compressor pressure ratio |

### Audit columns — the ones that make the window checks falsifiable

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `t_start` | window start, seconds into the drive |
| `t_span` | **real wall-clock span** of the window |
| `max_gap` | largest interval inside the window |
| `source` | which drive |
| `_merged` | how many raw windows de-duplicated into this point |

**Why these exist.** `steady_points()` used to size its window in *samples*, from
each drive's average rate. On a drive whose rate is not constant that is not a
60-second window: measured spans ran **42.8 to 73.7 seconds**, and one window
contained an internal hole of **3.18 s**.

A window is now rejected unless its span is within **±20 %** of `WINDOW_S = 60 s`
(`SPAN_TOL = 0.20`) and it contains no gap larger than **4× the median interval**
(`MAX_HOLE_X = 4.0`). Every surviving point records `t_span` and `max_gap`, so
the check is auditable rather than asserted.

Effect: 20 points became 17, `fb988991` contributed none, and the pooled residual
fell from 4.4 % to 2.3 %. <!-- RETIRED-OK -->
*(Those are the figures of the moment the checks were added. The eighth drive
then took the set to 22 points, where the residual reads 2.8 % fitted and 1.4 %
derived. The paragraph is kept as the record of what the window checks did.)*

> **Be honest about that.** Part of the improvement is the removal of the worst
> drive. The exclusion rule is legitimate only because it was written from a
> measurable defect (logger gaps) rather than from the residual.

### Coverage, and what it excludes

- **31–82 kPa manifold pressure.** Steady points need steady driving, and steady
  driving is light-load driving.
- Compressor flow bins: only **0.33–0.36 kg/s** remains empty, and the MAF sensor
  cannot reach it.
- The boosted, knock-limited, enriched region is **not reachable from a steady
  drive** and is validated against published correlations instead.

## `data/master_samples.csv` — 46 707 per-sample rows

25 columns. **This is what the ECU calibration is fitted to** — lambda and spark
versus operating point, plus the compressor operating line. Sample-level, because
calibration needs the scatter, not the means.

`source` · `t` · `rpm` · `map_kpa` · `v_kmh` · `t_amb` · `load_pct` · `air_gps` ·
`lam` · `spark` · `iat_pre_c` · `ect_c` · `oil_c` · `rad_out_c` · `tps` · `gear` ·
`wastegate` · `spark_tgt` · `oil_filt_c` · `boost_tgt` · `trq_wheel` ·
`corr_flow` · `press_ratio` · **`stable`** · **`maf_pinned`**

### The two flag columns

```python
maf_pinned = air_kgh >= 1019.9
stable     = (d_air < 40) & (d_bst < 0.6) & (~maf_pinned)
```

**`maf_pinned` — 517 samples across 5 drives.** `Air mass flow` tops out at
exactly **1020.0 kg/h**, the same number on every drive that reaches it. That is
a sensor ceiling. A pinned sample under-reports air, so manifold pressure
inverted from it comes out low and the compressor pressure ratio comes out high —
exactly at the top of the envelope, where the fit is most exposed.

They are excluded from `stable` but **kept in the file**, so the thesis can state
the limit rather than hide it.

> They are **not repaired** by substituting `Air mass flow participating in
> combustion`. That is the ECU's modelled trapped charge — a different quantity,
> median ratio **1.095** over the 517 pinned samples — and splicing two
> definitions puts a step in the curve. (It read 1.163 on seven drives.)

**`stable`** additionally requires the air and boost signals to be quiet. 43 853
quasi-steady samples sit behind the compressor fit; the highest pressure ratio
observed is **2.52**.

## Provenance of the calibrations fitted from this data

| calibration | fitted from | sample support |
|---|---|---|
| `BaselineECU` enrichment (v4) | eight drives | 422 / 168 / 465 samples in the three rpm bands above 200 kPa |
| `BaselineECU` spark, below ~90 kPa | 11 measured steady points | residual RMS 1.66° |
| `BaselineECU` spark, above ~90 kPa | **the plant's own Douaud-Eyzat knock limit** | not a fit |
| `thermal.ua_block_oil = 800 W/K` | 80 min of oil and coolant, 3 drives | median gap −1.2 K, p95 +5.4 K |
| `MAP_CEIL_KPA = 250` | 43 853 quasi-steady samples | max pressure ratio 2.52 |
| radiator `ua_rad_*` | **nothing — not identifiable** | R² = 0.157, negative ram coefficient |

### The enrichment fit has been wrong three times

| version | behaviour | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| v1, guessed | enriches from 120 kPa | far too early |
| v2, one 42-min drive | never enriches | only 4 s of high-load data |
| v3, two drives | stoichiometric to 230 kPa, then 0.85 | right effect, **wrong variable** |
| **v4, eight drives** | function of engine speed and sustained dwell | **current** |

v3 was fitted to **17 seconds** above 230 kPa. At 198 seconds the correlation
between λ and manifold pressure is **−0.05 — none at all**.

> **Two lessons, both cheap to relearn expensively: one drive is not evidence,
> and a fit that reproduces the effect can still be built on the wrong
> variable.** Check how many samples support a fit, and check that the variable
> you fitted against actually correlates.

## When a new drive CSV arrives

```bash
cp <new>.csv logs/raw/
python build_dataset.py "logs/raw/*.csv"      # rebuilds all three files
python compare_log.py data/master_points.csv  # re-scores the model
```

Then check, in this order — each of these prints:

1. **Did the sanity floor or the fuel-cut filter drop anything?**
2. **Did the operating-point count go up?** Zero new distinct points means the
   drive was not steady enough. That is a driving problem, not a code one.
3. **Did any window get rejected for span or a logger gap?** A drive that loses
   every window that way was logged with too many channels selected.
4. **Which compressor flow bins are still empty?**
5. **Does the load residual stay near 1.4 %?** A jump means the drive covers a
   region the model has not seen — information, not failure.
6. **Did the new drive push any channel to a flat maximum?** A flat maximum
   repeated across drives is a saturated sensor.

If the drive changes a calibration, **check the sample support and check that the
variable actually correlates** before accepting it.
