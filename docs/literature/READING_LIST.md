# Reading list: the literature review and the simulator's validation

Checked 21 October 2026. 183 sources for the literature review and for the systematic validation of the simulator. Each one has a citation, a working link, a free full-text link where one exists, the way to get it from Jeddah, its price if it has to be bought, its priority, and the reason the project needs it.

`references.bib`, beside this file, holds the same 183 sources for Zotero (188 entries, because two rows of the list are several EPA datasets each).

The list was compiled by Claude (an AI assistant) on 21 October 2026 from four checks, one per topic: preview theory, powertrain and thermal management, RL and engine modelling, and validation. No source appears twice: the 127 DOIs given for the sources are all different, and all 131 DOIs in the list (with the four given for related versions) resolved at doi.org on 21 October, each to the work named. Links and prices were checked with download tools and a scripted browser. A second, adversarial check on the same day re-checked every BUY and SDL-UNCONFIRMED link, every free full-text link and every price page, and added five sources the first version lacked; "What the second check changed" lists the result. No team member has read these sources yet; that is step 4 below. In the list, "opened" means the full text itself was downloaded or rendered and checked to be the paper. "Answered" or "returns 200" means only that the server responded. Prices are in US dollars as displayed, before any tax added at checkout. SAR figures use the fixed rate of 3.75 SAR per dollar.

**MUST** means the review's argument or the validation method rests on the source. **SHOULD** means it gives context. **UNVERIFIED** means nobody has confirmed the item, and it is written next to the exact claim it applies to.

**4. Do not cite anything until someone has read the full text.** An abstract, a summary, a line in this list or an AI's reading does not count. Each source gets one named reader before it is cited. Record it in Zotero with a tag such as `read-by-<name>` and the date. The "Why it is needed" column says what each source is expected to support. Check that against the text. If the paper does not say it, do not cite it for that point, and record what it does say. REFERENCES.md's status words (CONFIRMED, PARTIAL, UNVERIFIED) apply to these sources as well.

**5. Keep the PDFs outside the repository.** Use a local folder outside git, for example `Documents\GRAD-literature\`, and name each file by its id (for example `A03_Tomizuka-Whitney-1975.pdf`). Zotero's own storage also works. Never commit the PDFs. Most are licensed to the person who downloaded them, not to the project, and publishers forbid passing them on. Git will not stop you: `.gitignore` does not exclude `*.pdf`, and `DOC/` already tracks the team's own PDFs. Check `git status` before every commit.

**6. Import `references.bib` into Zotero** with File > Import. Each item comes in with tags for its list id, MUST or SHOULD, its access route and its topic. A child note gives the price and the free link. Filter on the tags `MUST` and `BUY` to see the shopping list.

## Corrections found during the checks

These belong in REFERENCES.md and in the review. This file does not change those files.

1. **REFERENCES.md section 7, item 6**, "Basir, Alaviyoun & Rosen 2022", has the wrong first author. It is Savaripour, H., Alaviyoun, S., & Rosen, M. A., *Clean Technologies* 4(2), 329–344, doi:10.3390/cleantechnol4020019 (C61).
2. **Burke, Olmeda, Arnau & Reyes-Belmonte 2014** (the London turbocharger conference paper) is no longer "abstract only". The University of Bath hosts a 12-page pre-print (C48).
3. **Heywood.** The archive.org scan the project used is a user upload in the "folkscanomy" collection, with no licence. For page numbers that can be cited, use AccessEngineering, a library copy or the Internet Archive lending copy (C36).
4. **Liu & Seiler 2026 (A31) does give a rate.** The bound is geometric, ∝ α^(p+1)/(1−α), on p. 9. Semantic Scholar and DBLP list the paper at the 2026 American Control Conference. It is not in Crossref, so the venue is UNVERIFIED. The earlier notes said "no rate" and "not peer reviewed".
5. **Wei et al. 2026 (A25)** states its rule, "set control preview to ≈0.7–1.5 τf", in units of the fast time scale τf = 5.5 h. That is not the lumped τ = 25.7 h.
6. **Which τ is meant.** In the theory, the time scale comes from the closed-loop matrix or from the actuator limits (A11, A15, A17, A31). The industrial rule of Shridhar & Cooper (A28) ties the horizon to the open-loop τ (H ≈ 5τ plus the dead time, as restated in A34; A28 itself has not been opened yet). The review has to say which τ the H/τ ratio uses.
7. **SDL.** The American Chemical Society is in SDL's A–Z list of databases, although the access notes left it out. SDL's two publisher filters are different lists, one for e-journals and one for e-books. IET and AccessEngineering appear only in the e-book filter.
8. **Sandia's PCMM report (D08).** Its title page lists the authors as Oberkampf, Pilch and Trucano. OSTI and Crossref give them as Oberkampf, Trucano and Pilch. The list and `references.bib` follow the title page.
9. **Names and pages.**
   - Crossref spells Reyes-Belmonte as "Rayes-Belmote" for Burke et al. 2016 (C58). Bath's header on the 2014 pre-print (C48) spells it "Rayes-Belmonte". `references.bib` uses Reyes-Belmonte.
   - Codevilla et al. 2019 (C24) has pages 9329–9338 in the CVF copy and 9328–9337 in IEEE Xplore. `references.bib` uses IEEE's, because its DOI points there.
10. **Serrano et al. 2010 (C56).** OpenAlex marks it as free to read, but the IOP page says it needs a subscription.
11. **NASA-HDBK-7009B (D06).** Every page header reads "DRAFT", although the cover says the handbook is approved. Cite it as provisional, with its date.
12. **No review of RL for IC-engine control from 2024 to 2026 was found.** Aliramezani et al. 2022 (C08) is the closest. The two newer reviews (C10, C11) cover energy management.
