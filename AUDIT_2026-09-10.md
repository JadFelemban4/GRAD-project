## Part 2 — the derived k does not measure what the documents say it measures

The derivation `k = 269.6 / T_in` is arithmetically correct and the three constants
are defined. The problem is what the resulting residual actually tests.

### 2.1 The plant cancels out of the comparison entirely

`compare_log.py:114` obtains manifold pressure from `map_from_airflow`, which at
`plant.py:437-445` inverts the exact relation `run_cycle` uses at `plant.py:164-167`.
So `load_model` reduces to the measured air mass per cylinder per cycle times
`R_AIR·T_in / vd_cyl`, and multiplying by `269.6 / T_in` cancels the temperature too.
What survives is the measured air mass over the DIN reference charge. Volumetric
efficiency, residual fraction and intake temperature all disappear.

| test | residual |
|---|---|
| derived form as the script prints it | 1.3737 % |
| model-free: MAF ÷ (vd_cyl · rho_DIN) | 1.3740 % |
| intake temperature raised 30 K at every point | 1.3738 % |
| intake temperature held at 300 K | 1.3738 % |
| volumetric efficiency forced to 0.5 everywhere | 1.3740 % |

The 1.4 % is therefore the ECU's `Relative air filling` channel against the ECU's
`Air mass flow` channel, through the DIN constants and the displacement. It contains
no cycle-model physics. The old fitted 2.8 % was the same kind of number; the fall
to 1.4 % is the removal of a spurious intake-temperature factor the fitted form
carried, not a physics gain.

`compare_log.py:139-145` already prints exactly this caveat for the air-mass row
(`the error is zero for arithmetic reasons, not physical ones`). The load row is the
same computation one algebraic step further and carries no such caveat; it prints
`HEADLINE ... PASS (zero fitted parameters)` at `compare_log.py:214-215`.

**Consequence for the wording.** `CLAUDE.md:47`, `validation_table.md` section B, and
the deck (slide 2 `Validated plant ... 1.4% load error`, slide 3 `MODEL AGAINST
MEASUREMENT`, notes 3 `the model reproduces the load trend`) all describe it as the
model reproducing the car. It is a channel-definition and unit-consistency check.

Honest replacement wording:

> The ECU's relative air filling equals the measured air mass per cylinder per cycle
> referred to the DIN reference density, with a +1.4 % bias and 0.8 % scatter over
> 22 points at 31–82 kPa, with no free parameters. That pins the channel definition
> and the displacement. It does not test the breathing model.

### 2.2 There is no part-load vehicle test of `volumetric_efficiency()` anywhere

Both logged pressure channels sit before the throttle. At the 22 steady points, with
the throttle at 14–20 %:

| quantity | range |
|---|---|
| inverted manifold pressure | 31.4 – 81.6 kPa |
| `map_raw`, psi to kPa | 92.7 – 96.9 kPa |
| ambient plus boost, psi to kPa | 100.8 – 124.6 kPa |

Mean absolute error against those channels is 44 % and 52 %. The sentence in
`CLAUDE.md`'s limitations saying the two agree inside the 4.4 % residual below
80 kPa is not supported by the data, and no script prints that figure. The breathing
model meets the car only under boost, which is section 2.3.

### 2.3 The intake temperature sensor reads compressor-outlet air

On the B58 the air enters through the throttle body into the manifold that houses
the integrated water-to-air charge cooler, so *before the throttle* is *before the
cooler*. The 163 °C the channel reaches under boost matches a compressor outlet at
pressure ratio 2.3 and 70 % efficiency from 40 °C air; no working cooler passes that
to the cylinders. A separate `Temperature after the intercooler` channel exists and
is dead in the census (`logs/CHANNEL_CENSUS.md:135`).

`plant.py:166` uses this sensor as in-cylinder charge temperature, and
`logs/CHANNEL_SET_FINAL.md:44` labels it *post-intercooler*, which appears to be
wrong. Effect on the boosted inversion, over 203 unpinned samples:

| charge temperature used | inverted manifold pressure |
|---|---|
| the sensor, median 117 °C | 295 kPa |
| 50 °C | 241 kPa |
| 40 °C | 233 kPa |
| logged: 19.27 psi gauge over 14.23 psi ambient | 231 kPa |

The 28 % boost gap that `CLAUDE.md` attributes to volumetric efficiency and to
sensor lag is explained almost entirely by the temperature. This does not touch the
1.4 %, because the temperature cancels there. It does touch every boosted prediction
the model makes: spark, exhaust gas temperature, and the map.

**Source note.** The manifold-integrated cooler layout is documented by the
aftermarket manifold vendors ([CSF](https://news.csfrace.com/new-csf-gen-1-b58-charge-air-cooler-manifold/),
[Go-Parts](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/engine-intake-manifold-bmw-x3-bmw-x4-bmw-z4-2017-2025));
a BMW service document would be the stronger citation and was not obtained.

### 2.4 The stated limit names a mechanism that cannot operate

`compare_log.py:187-190` and `validation_table.md` say k varies with the pre-throttle
sensor, which lags under boost, so the form should not be carried into boost. The
derived residual is independent of that sensor at every load, as the table in 2.1
shows. The conclusion is right and the reason is wrong. The real limits are the MAF
ceiling at 1020.0 kg/h, where the ECU reports 176 % filling while the pinned MAF
gives 158 %, and the channel refresh skew in section 2.6.

### 2.5 The "0.1 % agreement" is a coincidence

Model-free ratio of the ECU channel to the direct calculation:

```
rl_ECU / rl_direct = 1.0138 +/- 0.003     bias +1.4 %, scatter 0.8 %
implied reference temperature = 277 K
a 20 C reference would leave 5.9 %
```

Algebraically, `k_fit / mean(k_pred) = (C/DIN) x (T_harm/T_eff) x E`, which evaluates
to `1.0138 x 0.989 x 0.998 = 1.0009`. The 0.989 factor is forced by a 0.70
correlation between load and intake temperature. Had the ECU matched DIN exactly,
the script would have printed a 1.1 % disagreement instead.

Defensible wording: *consistent with a 0 °C reference within 1.4 %, and 20 °C is
excluded at 5.9 %.* Stronger still, cite Bosch rather than inferring the reference
state from the fit. Bosch defines relative air charge as the in-cylinder fresh-air
mass referred to 1013 hPa and 273 K, in patent
[EP1015746B1](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP1015746B1/en) and in *Gasoline-engine
management: basics and components*, 2nd ed. 2001. The ECU's own combustion-air
channel implies 270.3 K, so the two channels bracket 273 K.

### 2.6 The logger writes one parameter per row

In the three large drives, every channel changes value only every 4.6 to 6.0 seconds;
the rows at roughly 4.5 Hz are carried-forward values.

| drive | rows per second | seconds between real value changes |
|---|---|---|
| `cb67b01f` | 4.56 | 5.04 |
| `7475b5d7` | 4.34 | 6.00 |
| `3aca2ec1` | 4.74 | 4.56 |

So "twenty channels log cleanly at 4.6 Hz" is true of rows, not of channels. A sixty
second window holds about ten real values per channel. Harmless at steady load, but
it explains the boosted scatter: air mass and filling are sampled seconds apart
during a pull.

## Part 3 — what v16 genuinely improved

The derived form exposes the displacement, and the fitted form was blind to it. A
four-cylinder geometry gives a 48 % error under the derived k, while the fitted k
absorbs it completely and still reports 2.8 % with the constant moving to 0.52.
So `CLAUDE.md`'s account of how Phase B escaped the geometry mistake is true of the
derived form only.

The reading of the ECU channel as DIN-normalised is correct and is supported by the
literature, independently of this project's data.

## Part 4 — smaller items, each verified

- `compare_log.py:218-220` tells the reader that a large load residual means to look
  at volumetric efficiency first. That quantity cancels, so the residual cannot grow
  for that reason. The advice is unreachable.
- `verify_docs.py` does not assert the 1.4 % or the 2.8 % at all. Its load block
  checks 269.6, the mean derived k of 0.783, and the excluded 0.840.
- `compare_log.py --map-from-log` scores zero rows on `data/master_points.csv`: the
  alias table has no entry for the manifold-pressure channel under the short schema,
  so every row is dropped, the table prints empty, and the script still exits 0.
  `CLAUDE.md` says this mode exists to reproduce the pre-throttle failure for the
  thesis; it cannot do so on the canonical dataset.
- `validate.py:185` still prints that there is no compressor flow ceiling, although
  `plant.boost_ceiling_kpa` exists and `CLAUDE.md` says it bounds the model.
- `check_map.py` schedules lambda by load, which mistake 4 identifies as the wrong
  variable. The effect is at most one degree per cell and no cell becomes `knk`.
- The count of six unreachable cells rests on a 0.24 kPa margin at 3000 rpm and
  220 kPa, which is inside the ceiling fit's own residual. A refit could make it 7.
- The cell at 6000 rpm and 40 kPa reads 45 because that is the top of the search
  range, not because 45 is the best-torque advance.
