B58 preview study

When is it worth knowing the road ahead? The project's claim is that the answer is set by one ratio, H/τ: how far ahead you can see, over how fast the protected part heats up. This page shows what the simulation says on the locked scenario, and every place the simulator can be laid beside the car's own logs.

Phase D on the locked scenario

A 900 s motorway climb in 42 °C air: three minutes flat, then a 12 % grade at 130 km/h. The turbine housing is protected from 850 °C, the knee of the damage model exp((T − 1123)/45). Every policy is scored on the same 20 frozen episodes from evaluate.py.

The premise, one change at a time

Six changes to the plant on 28 September moved the baseline's damage from 951.9 to 920.1. Preview over current-grade stayed between −0.4 and −0.3 points at every step.

The hand-written policies on the locked climb after each change, in order. Step 4 raised the baseline because the exhaust flow had been fuel × 15, well short of air + fuel; step 6 lowered it again because 42 °C air is thinner than the 1.2 kg/m³ that had been typed, so the car needs less power. This is a record: each step needs the code as it stood at that step, so it cannot be regenerated (compare_calibration.py).

The H/τ sweep, against both comparators

Sweeping the turbine housing's heat capacity moves τ from 6 to 472 s against a 30 s horizon. Where the limit binds (3 of 5 values), preview over current-grade is −0.03 to −0.01 points. The 13–21 points over the reactive policy are timing, not preview.

predictive minus reactive predictive minus current-grade: the preview measurement

Hand-written policies, one rollout each, scored with exp((T − 1123)/45); τ = C/UA at the climb's own exhaust flow of 121.3 g/s. A predictive policy written by hand that acts on the grade ahead lands on one that acts on the grade now (AUDIT.md C3), so this sweep cannot confirm or refute the criterion. Re-scored under six cost curvatures at τ = 47 s, preview over current-grade is never more than 0.04 points from zero; with the limit re-set per τ to keep it binding (H2b), at most +0.3. The trained ablation on varied roads is the test.

Knock

○untested

The model's knock integral shows no relationship with the retard the car applies: correlation −0.119 over 14 318 rows. Those rows hold only 404 real readings of each ignition angle, one every 8 s. A knock retard lasts a second or two, so this test could not have seen it either way.

car samples, darker = more damage-term knee, KI 0.85

Where the model says knock (KI above 0.85) the car's median retard is −3.0°; where it says none, 0.0°. 32 % of the paired readings were taken more than a second apart. This is the trap of mistake 13b, where a real relationship read +0.35 until faster logging showed +0.95. Until drive C logs both angles fast, do not quote a knock-limited spark or the knock damage term as calibrated, or as refuted. 118 rows above 20° are gearshift torque cuts and sit off the top.

The simulator never lets boost exceed the ceiling, which since 28 September is the measured envelope itself, with no formula. The highest pressure ratio logged is 2.52. Drive B shows the envelope is still too low at 1600–2000 rpm.

89 genuine full-throttle boost readings from drive B, each paired with engine speed interpolated to the moment it was read (a +55 rpm median shift from the last reading), against the manifold pressure the modelled engine reaches at the ceiling. Part 3 · for the car

Drives we need

Three drives, A to C; B is done, and since 29 September C matters most for Phase D and A for the thermal model. They are lettered because the project's "Drive 1" is the completed first drive of 6 September. Each settles one question the logs cannot. Phase D does not wait for them. Log only, never write to the car; the passenger runs the phone. Skip any step that can't be done legally and safely. Full notes are in logs/DRIVE_PLAN.md.

Before every drive

  1. Select only the listed channels. Fewer channels are read more often: 7 refresh every 1.45 s, 26 every 7.5 s.
  2. Always include Coolant temperature. Without it the whole drive is filtered out.
  3. Warm the engine first, to a coolant temperature of 88 °C or more.
  4. Start the log and wait 30 seconds before driving; the first rows are placeholder zeros.
  5. Afterwards, copy the CSV into logs/raw/ with a descriptive name and run python new_drive.py on it.

A · A long mountain climb, for heat

★most valuable for the thermal model

The car has never been logged under sustained load: only 2.8 % of samples reach the climb's load. It is the data the oil rows of the validation table still need, and the coolant regulation law, and it adds the first sustained-load coolant data and a hot idle. Log ambient temperature too. It changes the plant, so do it before the retrain or retrain again after it.

Channels (8): Engine speed, Vehicle speed, Air mass flow, Lambda actual value, Coolant temperature, Oil temperature, Engine radiator outlet temperature (coolant), Ambient temperature.

  1. The longest continuous climb within reach, such as an escarpment road up from the coast: 15–25 minutes without stopping.
  2. In the afternoon, when it is hottest.
  3. Start the log at the foot of the climb. Climb at a steady, legal speed in D.
  4. At the top, park safely and leave the engine idling for 5 minutes with the log running. Do not switch it off; this tests the model's thermostat against the car's heat-management valve.

B · Full-throttle roll-ons in a high gear, for low-rpm boost

✓done 28 Sep

Logged and in the dataset. The boost ceiling is now the measured envelope: within a few per cent of the car's highest boost from 2000 rpm up, still −20 % at 1600–2000 rpm (Compressor ceiling). It did not retire the gearbox workaround: with no torque channel, the pulls can't say whether the real car would hold the gear. It logged no ambient temperature, so every plan on this page now lists that channel.

How it was driven

Channels (7): Engine speed, Vehicle speed, Air mass flow, Boost pressure, Ambient pressure, Throttle valve angle related to the lower stop, Coolant temperature.

  1. A straight, empty road with a limit of 120 km/h or more. Gearbox in M; hold the gear with the paddles.
  2. From a steady speed, floor it for 3–4 seconds and lift, always before the limit: 6th from 70 km/h (about 1770 rpm), 7th from 90 km/h (about 1870 rpm), 8th from 100 km/h (about 1620 rpm).
  3. Each three times, with a minute of steady cruising between pulls so the logger gets fresh readings.

C · Loaded, steady-gear driving in the heat, for knock

★most valuable for Phase D

The trained agents run spark to just under the knock model's knee, and with that forbidden they fall to current-grade's level, so whether the car tolerates that spark decides how much of their advantage is real. The old drive read each ignition angle only every 8 s, too slowly to see a knock event. This one logs as few channels as possible so both are read every ~1.25 s, with no gearshifts in the way.

Channels (6): Engine speed, Air mass flow, Actual ignition angle, Target ignition angle from torque intervention, Coolant temperature, Ambient temperature. Speed and lambda are left out on purpose: in a held gear the gear is known, and below 3300 rpm lambda is 1.0.

  1. A second ascent of the same climb, in the afternoon heat, gearbox in M.
  2. Pick a gear that keeps the engine at 2000–3500 rpm: low speed and high load is where an engine knocks.
  3. Hold steady, firm throttle for 20–30 s, with no gear change inside a segment. Aim for ten or more segments.

What no drive on this car can settle

The turbine temperature and the 850 °C trigger: every pre-catalyst exhaust channel reads zero, so it needs a published source. Enrichment at high rpm and long dwell: needs seconds of full load at 5000+ rpm, which isn't legal on a road; it's a track-day job. A real boost leak: not something to induce on a borrowed car.

What this page is not

Phase D's first run, not its last

The 20 agents trained on 29 September at 130 km/h on a new road every episode, at dt 1.0, on the plant derived from the logs. Still open, and each would change the plant and so need a retrain: the boost ceiling low at 1600–2000 rpm, and drive A. The team retrains after every drive. The fuel is settled: 95 RON, what the model runs.

Hand-written rows are one rollout

They do not read the preference weights, so their zero spread is structural. It is not a sign of stability.

Turbine temperature is modelled

No sensor on this car reads it. Its heat capacity, c_turb = 6000 J/K, is assumed, and it sets τ, the denominator of the claim.

Altitude is not modelled

The scored climb rises 2 340 m, but the engine breathes sea-level air (101.3 kPa) the whole way; a standard atmosphere would give about 76 kPa at the top. Read the scenario as sustained heavy load at sea level, not as a mountain.

One workaround, stated

When the modelled engine falls short of the torque asked for, the gearbox kicks down. Drive B showed the ceiling is still too low at 1600–2000 rpm, so the model runs a gear lower there than the car may need. There is no torque channel to settle it, so the workaround stays. The locked climb runs above that band.

The H/τ sweep is hand-written

It shows preview worth nothing over current-grade, but a hand-written predictive policy cannot do anything current-grade does not. It neither confirms nor refutes the criterion; only the trained pair can.
